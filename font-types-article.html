Font Types...what they are and how to use them.

Typography plays a key role in creating quality websites, apps, emails, eBooks, and more. It involves creating or selecting a system of typefaces and fonts to express the written word. This creative art form can evoke strong emotions. Use visually appealing letters and arrange type to express your brand’s personality.

Well-crafted typography can enhance clear communication, legibility, and readability.

Below each Font Type sketch on this page are links to the fonts that were used in the descriptive examples.

Serif Fonts:

These are the oldest types of fonts, with the first examples appearing as early as the late 15th century. The word ‘serif’ refers to the small feet present at the tops and bottoms of each letter. These tiny flourishes stemmed from artists’ brushes and would be added to the letters as decorative elements.

Today Serif fonts are among the most popular typefaces in use and are considered to be very readable. They are often used in longer printed texts such as books, magazines, documents, and even logos…with styles like ‘Times New Roman’ being ever-present.

Some of the well-known Serif fonts include Georgia, Times New Roman, Palatino, and Garamond.

Google Fonts_Garamond

Sans Serif Fonts:

This simple, modern typeface does not include the flourishes found in the Serif typeface. Offering a clean, modern, and minimalist aesthetic, it is highly legible on digital screens. Popular for web design, branding, and user interfaces, Open Sans is recognized as one of the most readable fonts and often considered to be one of the best fonts for websites; designed with wide letterforms and generous spacing that makes it easy to view on any screen. The font's neutral style has made it a favorite among designers seeking accessible fonts that work well across platforms.

Sans serif typeface examples include Helvetica, Roboto, Open Sans, and Montserrat.

Google Fonts_Open Sans

Slab Serif Fonts:

A Slab Serif font, also known as an Egyptian serif or just “slab”, is a distinct typeface style characterized by bold, block-like serifs at the ends of strokes. Despite the name, Slab Serifs have no direct connection to Egypt but instead reflect a timeless strength that made them ideal for large and impactful displays in posters and advertisements.

Slab Serif fonts gained popularity during the early 19th century, particularly for advertising and print media, thanks to their ability to grab attention and convey strength. In the digital age, Slab Serif fonts evolved, balancing their historical charm with modern design requirements, making them a preferred choice in branding, editorial layouts, and user interfaces. Though it’s legible in small sizes, it’s best suited for headlines, logos, and other larger design elements. Its geometric appearance has a unique style that can add visual appeal to your website and help to create a memorable brand identity.

Top Slab Serif fonts include Rockwell, Clarendon, and Roboto Slab.

Google Fonts_Roboto Slab

Script Fonts:

The origins of script fonts date back to classical calligraphy among the hand-lettered manuscripts from the Roman and Renaissance times. This fluid, cursive typeface can range from elegant calligraphy to playful handwriting styles. Not ideal for body text, a script typeface works best for quotes, signatures, and short headers.

Examples include Snell Roundhand, Scriptina,and Pacifico.

Google Fonts_Pacifico

Display Fonts:

Also known as decorative, this attention-grabbing typeface is engineered for large-scale, high-impact usage. Its primary role is to immediately attract attention and help brands express their identity and create strong visual impressions. They convey a specific mood or tone – from vintage aesthetics to modern minimalism. Typically 14 points or higher, making them ideal for headlines, logos, posters, and brand identities rather than body text.

These expressive, often unique, and stylized fonts are designed to capture immediate attention and set a specific mood or aesthetic. They are crucial for creating a long-lasting impact and help define a brand’s voice – bold and edgy, elegant and sophisticated, or playful and creative

Display typeface examples include Abril Fatface and pairing it with Lato for body text.

Google Fonts_Abril Fatface

Monospace Fonts:

This retro, typewriter-style typeface displays fixed-width letters and spacing, so that each word’s character takes up the same width. Used in source code for easier readability, monospace typeface is used across numerous websites and graphic designs.

Monospace fonts invoke a retro, tech-oriented vibe: their design style is ideally suited for programming fonts or for something like a vintage video game website.

Examples include Courier, Bergen Mono, and Source Code Pro.

Google Fonts_Source Code Pro


Ultimately, typography is not just about choosing a font; it is about organizing visual information to make the reader's experience enjoyable and effective.
